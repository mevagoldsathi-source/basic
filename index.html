How to know it’s real

Saffron is the most adulterated spice in the world. Three things you can check at home, with any brand — including ours.

01

The cold-water test

Drop a few threads in cold water. Real kesar releases a golden-yellow colour slowly, over several minutes, while the threads stay red. Dyed threads bleed red straight away.

02

Aroma & taste

Genuine saffron smells floral, almost like honey and hay, and tastes faintly bitter. If it smells of nothing or tastes sweet, something has been added.

03

How to use it

Soak 8–10 threads in two tablespoons of warm milk or water for 15–20 minutes, then add the whole infusion to your biryani, kheer, kahwa or phirni.

One flower, three threads

Each crocus gives only three crimson stigmas, and every one is picked by hand within hours of the flower opening, in the early light of an October morning. It takes well over a hundred thousand flowers to make a single kilo.

Pampore’s karewa soil and cold, clear autumns are why Kashmiri saffron carries a Geographical Indication tag — and why its colour and aroma are unlike any other.
